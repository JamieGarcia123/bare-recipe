Essential Spices Every Home Cook Needs (Plus Easy DIY Seasoning Blends)

Build Your Spice Cabinet Without Breaking the Bank

When you're just starting to cook, it can feel like every recipe calls for a different spice. Before long, you're standing in the grocery store wondering if you really need to spend $6 on a tiny jar you'll only use once.

The good news? You don't.

A well-stocked spice cabinet doesn't have to be expensive, and you don't need dozens of seasonings to make flavorful meals. With a handful of versatile spices and a little creativity, you can create countless flavor combinations right at home.

Start With the Basics

If you're building your spice collection from scratch, begin with a few essentials that work in almost every kitchen.

Consider keeping these on hand:

  • Salt
  • Black pepper
  • Garlic powder
  • Onion powder
  • Paprika
  • Smoked paprika
  • Chili powder
  • Ground cumin
  • Italian seasoning
  • Oregano
  • Basil
  • Parsley
  • Cinnamon
  • Red pepper flakes

These simple spices can season everything from roasted vegetables and baked chicken to soups, pasta, tacos, and eggs.

Buy in Bulk and Save Money

One of the easiest ways to save money is to skip the expensive grocery store spice aisle.

Many grocery stores have a bulk section where you can buy exactly the amount you need. International grocery stores often have larger packages of common spices at much lower prices than traditional supermarkets.

Buying in bulk means:

  • Lower cost per ounce
  • Less packaging waste
  • You only buy what you'll actually use
  • It's easy to refill containers instead of replacing them

Store Them in Glass Jars

Instead of keeping spices in mismatched plastic containers, transfer them into matching glass jars.

Glass jars:

  • Keep spices fresher longer
  • Are easy to label
  • Look great on open shelves
  • Make it easier to see when you're running low

A simple label with the spice name and the purchase date can also help you remember when it's time to replace older spices.

Make Your Own Seasoning Blends

Store-bought seasoning packets are convenient, but they're often loaded with extra salt, sugar, and preservatives.

Making your own blends lets you control the flavor while saving money.

Taco Seasoning

Mix together:

  • 2 tablespoons chili powder
  • 1 tablespoon cumin
  • 2 teaspoons paprika
  • 1 teaspoon garlic powder
  • 1 teaspoon onion powder
  • 1 teaspoon oregano
  • ½ teaspoon black pepper
  • ½ teaspoon salt

Italian Seasoning

Combine:

  • Oregano
  • Basil
  • Thyme
  • Rosemary
  • Parsley

Store in a sealed jar and use it for pasta sauce, chicken, vegetables, or homemade pizza.

BBQ Rub

Mix:

  • Brown sugar
  • Smoked paprika
  • Garlic powder
  • Onion powder
  • Black pepper
  • Salt
  • Chili powder
  • Cayenne (optional)

Perfect for ribs, pork, chicken, or roasted potatoes.

Label Everything

You'll thank yourself later.

Use waterproof labels or a label maker so every jar is easy to identify. It keeps your pantry organized and makes cooking much faster.

Don't Let Spices Go to Waste

Ground spices gradually lose their strength over time.

If a spice has very little aroma when you open the jar, it's probably time to replace it. Buying smaller amounts more often usually gives you fresher flavor than keeping a large container for years.

A Small Collection Goes a Long Way

You don't need fifty different spices to cook delicious meals.

Start with a handful of versatile favorites, buy them in bulk when you can, store them properly, and experiment with making your own blends. As your confidence grows, your spice collection can grow with it.

Cooking doesn't have to be complicated—or expensive. Sometimes all it takes is the right seasoning.
